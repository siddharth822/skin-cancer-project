MELANOMA / A GENERAL OVERVIEW

How melanoma stages are described

Within each stage, more detailed subgroups depend on pathology and other clinical findings.

0

In situ

Melanoma is limited to the outer skin layer.

I–II

Localized melanoma

Pathology, including tumor thickness and ulceration, helps distinguish localized stages.

III

Regional spread

Regional lymph nodes or nearby skin and lymphatic sites may be involved.

IV

Distant spread

Melanoma has spread to distant sites or organs.

Squamous cell carcinoma

SCC staging depends on the anatomic site, tumor characteristics and evidence of lymph-node or distant spread. A dermatologist determines the appropriate staging system.

Basal cell carcinoma

Most BCC cases are assessed by local extent and recurrence risk. Routine numerical staging is uncommon; advanced cases require specialist assessment.

What to discuss at your appointment

Ask whether a biopsy is needed, what the pathology shows, whether thickness or ulceration matters, and whether lymph-node assessment or other tests are indicated.
